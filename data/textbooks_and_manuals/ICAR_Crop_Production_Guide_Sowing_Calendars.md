## CHAPTER 1: NATIONAL AGRO-CLIMATIC ZONING & SEASONAL CLASSIFICATION

### 1.1 Seasonal Agricultural Framework in India
Indian agriculture operates across three primary seasonal cycles determined by the South-West and North-East monsoon regimes, ambient photoperiod, and soil temperature dynamics:

1. **Kharif Season (South-West Monsoon Crops):**
   - Sowing window: Commences with the onset of the South-West monsoon in May to July.
   - Harvesting period: September to December.
   - Primary crops: Paddy (Aman), Cotton, Soybean, Groundnut, Maize, Pigeonpea (Tur), Jowar, and Bajra.
   - Moisture dynamic: Relies predominantly on monsoon precipitation; soil water management requires in-situ moisture retention (Broad Bed and Furrow systems) and surface drainage in high-rainfall belts.

2. **Rabi Season (Post-Monsoon & Winter Crops):**
   - Sowing window: October to December after the cessation of monsoon rains, as soil and ambient temperatures decline.
   - Harvesting period: March to May.
   - Primary crops: Wheat, Barley, Mustard, Chickpea (Bengal Gram), Lentil, Rabi Onion, Potato, and Boro/Rabi Rice.
   - Moisture dynamic: Dependent on residual subsoil moisture and critical canal/tubewell supplemental irrigations. Vulnerable to terminal heat stress during grain filling in March.

3. **Zaid Season (Summer Season Crops):**
   - Sowing window: February to April in assured irrigation tracts.
   - Harvesting period: May to June.
   - Primary crops: Moong (Green Gram), Urad, Watermelon, Muskmelon, Cucumber, Tomato, and Summer Fodder.

## CHAPTER 2: COMMODITY SOWING CALENDARS & AGRO-CLIMATIC SPECIFICATIONS

### 2.1 Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum & Gossypium arboreum)
- **Agro-Climatic Belt:** Central Zone (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh) and Southern Zone (Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka).
- **Optimum Sowing Window:**
  - North India (Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan): Late April to mid-May. Sowing after May 25 is prohibited to avoid severe Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV) and early square attack by Pink Bollworm.
  - Central India (Vidarbha, Marathwada, Khandesh, Saurashtra): Mid-June to early July, strictly after receiving at least 75 to 100 mm of cumulative monsoon rains.
  - Southern India (Telangana, Rayalaseema, North Karnataka): June to July for Kharif rainfed crops; August to September in winter cotton zones.
- **Soil & Tilth Requirements:** Deep fertile black cotton soils (Vertisols) or well-drained medium loam soils with depth > 90 cm and neutral to slightly alkaline pH (6.5 to 8.2).
- **Seed Rate & Population Density:**
  - Bt Cotton Hybrids: 1.5 to 2.0 kg/acre (approx 1 packet of 450 g per 0.25 to 0.33 acre) spaced at 90 cm x 60 cm or 120 cm x 45 cm (approx 7,400 to 9,800 plants/ha).
  - High Density Planting System (HDPS) with compact non-Bt / Bt varieties: 80 cm x 20 cm or 60 cm x 15 cm with 5 to 7 kg seed/acre.

### 2.2 Jute (Corchorus olitorius & Corchorus capsularis)
- **Agro-Climatic Belt:** Lower Gangetic Plains (West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha, Meghalaya).
- **Optimum Sowing Window:**
  - White Jute (Corchorus capsularis): Mid-March to mid-April. Capsularis tolerates early water stagnation and is suited for low-lying and inundated areas.
  - Tossa Jute (Corchorus olitorius): Mid-April to mid-May. Olitorius produces superior, golden, tensile fiber but is sensitive to early waterlogging; requires upland, well-drained alluvial soil.
  - Sowing beyond late May severely compromises fiber length, cell wall lignification, and tensile strength.
- **Soil & Seedbed Preparation:** Alluvial sandy loam and clay loam soils with pH 6.0 to 7.5. Seedbed requires fine tilth achieved by 3 to 4 ploughings and cross-harrowing.
- **Seed Rate:** Line sowing with seed drill: 5 to 6 kg/ha for Tossa; 7 to 8 kg/ha for White Jute. Line spacing maintained at 25-30 cm with plant-to-plant thinning to 7-10 cm at 21 days after emergence.

### 2.3 Wheat (Triticum aestivum)
- **Agro-Climatic Belt:** North-Western Plains Zone (NWPZ: Punjab, Haryana, Western UP, Rajasthan) and North-Eastern Plains Zone (NEPZ: Eastern UP, Bihar).
- **Optimum Sowing Window:**
  - Timely Sown Irrigated Wheat: November 1 to November 20 is the golden agro-climatic window.
  - Late Sown Irrigated Wheat: November 25 to December 25.
  - Sowing after November 25 results in an irreversible yield penalty of 35 to 45 kg per hectare per day of delay due to accelerated phenology and terminal heat during March grain filling.
- **Seed Rate:** 100 kg/ha (40 kg/acre) for timely sown; 125 kg/ha for late sown and bold-grained varieties (e.g. DBW-187, DBW-222, HD-2967, PBW-550).
- **Row Spacing & Depth:** Row-to-row spacing 20 to 22.5 cm; sowing depth must not exceed 4 to 5 cm.

### 2.4 Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)
- **Agro-Climatic Belt:** Southern Plateau (Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu) and Central/Western Plains (Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat).
- **Optimum Sowing & Transplanting Windows:**
  - Kharif Crop: Nursery raised in June to July; field transplanting in July to August.
  - Rabi Crop: Nursery raised in October to November; field transplanting in November to December.
  - Summer Crop: Nursery raised in January to February; transplanting in February to March (requires assured drip irrigation).
- **Seed Rate:** 100 to 150 grams per acre for F1 hybrids (e.g. Arka Rakshak, Arka Samrat, US-440); 200 to 250 g/acre for open-pollinated varieties.
- **Transplanting Protocol:** 25 to 30 day old sturdy seedlings transplanted on raised beds spaced 60 cm x 45 cm for determinate varieties and 90 cm x 45 cm for staked indeterminate varieties.
